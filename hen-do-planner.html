Hen do planning

Plan the hen do.Keep the chaos somewhere else.

A hen do can go from “we should do a weekend away” to twelve separate conversations very quickly. Give the organiser one clear structure for dates, travel, accommodation, activities, money and the people actually coming.

21 September 20267 min readPractical guide

A good hen do does not need military precision. It does need one current version of the plan.

The usual problem is not a shortage of ideas. It is that the useful details are spread across messages, booking emails, screenshots and somebody’s Notes app. The organiser then becomes the search function for the entire group.

Start with the fixed points

Agree the date, destination, rough budget and likely guest list before disappearing into restaurant links and activity ideas. Those four decisions determine nearly everything that comes afterwards.

You do not need perfect certainty. You need enough agreement to book without rebuilding the trip every time somebody changes their mind.

Create one official plan

Once bookings begin, decide where the current version lives. Keep travel details, accommodation, transfers, booked activities, addresses and useful documents together.

If “what time is the flight?” means searching a three-month-old chat for the right screenshot, the system is already doing too much work.

Useful rule

Conversation can move quickly. The official plan should move only when the actual trip changes.

Make the money visible early

Hen weekends get awkward when one person has paid several deposits and nobody is completely sure what they still owe. Record each shared cost, who paid it, who should contribute and whether it is settled.

Keep group spend separate from personal spend. The villa deposit is a shared trip cost. Somebody’s airport coffee is not.

Turn opinions into decisions

Use polls when the group genuinely needs to choose: an activity, restaurant, departure time or room arrangement. Then move the result into the plan.

The group chat can continue discussing it. The official answer should not disappear back into twenty new messages.

Build the final briefing

In the final week, everyone should be able to see one concise version: where to be, when to be there, what is booked, what still needs paying and anything they actually need to bring.

The final briefing should come from information already stored, not from the organiser rebuilding the weekend from memory the night before.

During the weekend, make the system quieter

Once the trip starts, nobody wants another admin job. The useful information should already be there, with quick updates only when plans change.

This is also when photos and videos multiply. Decide where the shared record will live before everyone goes home with a different fragment of the weekend.

Questions people actually ask

What is the easiest way to organise a hen do?

Agree the date, destination, rough budget and guest list first. Then keep bookings, costs and decisions in one shared place so the organiser is not repeatedly rebuilding the plan from messages.

Can we still use WhatsApp?

Yes. Keep WhatsApp for conversation. Put the official bookings, costs, documents and final decisions somewhere designed to be checked again later.

How early should we start planning?

Start as soon as the core group can agree a realistic date and budget. The exact lead time depends on destination, group size and availability.
